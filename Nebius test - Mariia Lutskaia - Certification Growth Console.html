Certification Growth Console

Nebius wants 10,000 people to pass its AI certification exam by the end of 2026. The Builder Program and its certification are taken as the main program every campaign sends leads to. This is my plan for getting there with influencer marketing: which of the three approaches to fund, in what order, and how each funnel would be reviewed weekly. Every number is a base-case assumption.

The whole plan in three messages. Everything below is the reasoning behind them, one tab per approach.
01

Put the money on the two fastest, cheapest levers

1a (paid YouTube sponsorships) and the Certification Cup (a live exam event) get full weight. 1b (partnerships inside Udemy and Coursera courses) supports them. Approach 2 (universities and professional communities) is a small, gated bet. Approach 3 (an ambassador program) is planted now and pays off in 2027.

02

Promising on paper, so fund it in stages

The $400K influencer budget reaches ~9,200 of the 10,000 certified in the base case, about 800 short, so this plan alone does not close the goal. The range is 5,900 to 10,000 and rests on untested assumptions, so a funnel gets more budget only after proving its cost per certified.

03

The Cup is the original idea

A live, gamified exam where creators and viewers compete on one leaderboard. It fixes the biggest leak in a normal sponsored video: people register, then never sit the exam. Modeled at roughly 2.5× more certified from the same reach.

Sequencing
Q2 · The order, and why

1a launches week 1 because it's the fastest way to get real data. Approach 2 opens the same week, even though it's minor, because its 4–8 week deal cycle runs regardless of start date. Approach 3 has no clock; it gets seeded passively, through whichever creators the Cup brings together.

A "gate" is a scheduled go/no-go check: funnels that work keep or gain budget, the rest lose it.

Budget
Reviewing the portfolio, not one funnel at a time

One weekly rule decides where the money goes: the funnel with the lowest cost for its next increment of certified people gets the next dollar.

Each week, rank the live funnels by their recent cost per certified, counting only those with enough attempts to trust the number. Read it as the cost of the latest spend, not the running average, because cost rises as a funnel scales (the ~$60 scale-phase assumption builds that in), and cap each funnel at the creators it can actually book. In the first weeks, use a projected figure built from cost per registration and attempt rate, since few people have finished the exam yet. A funnel in "modify" gets one cycle to improve; one that hits "stop" loses its budget that day.

Approach 1 runs as two funnels: 1a (YouTube sponsorships) and 1b (partnerships inside Udemy and Coursera courses).

Creator management
How creators are picked and managed
Sourcing
Technical YouTube channels (cloud, ML, developer tutorials) in three tiers: a few large channels, more mid-size ones, and small ones to test cost per certified cheaply.
Vetting
Audience quality (real comments, engagement relative to views), audience geography against target regions, and overlap between creators. Channels with inflated numbers, such as sudden view spikes or views far out of line with subscribers, are dropped.
Brief and approval
One call to action and the key facts. The creator writes the script; Nebius approves facts, disclosure and brand safety before posting.
Terms
Flat fee, plus a bonus per certified at scale; usage rights so strong videos can be boosted as ads; disclosure; and a clause to pause the deal if a brand-safety issue appears.
Reporting
Every creator gets their own code and landing page and is reviewed on cost per certified. Winners get repeat deals, usually at a 20–35% premium; the rest rotate out.
Optional
The Certification Cup

A normal sponsored video leaks at one point: people register, then never sit the exam. The Cup closes that gap by design. Four to six creators take the same exam live, head to head, and their audiences take it with them.

Timed rounds, on-screen rank changes and one shared leaderboard put creators and viewers in the same contest. Beating a favorite creator is a reason to sit the exam, not just watch. Off camera, the creators meet, give feedback on the exam and become the founding cohort of Approach 3. It remains a bet, tested as a hypothesis.

Indirect evidence: Nebius already runs a competitive format, its Global AI Hackathon (over $50K in prizes). Comparable developer contests reportedly draw far more people, about 9,200 registrants for AWS's and 25,000 for RevenueCat's against about 3,500 for Nebius's (a third-party tracker, mid-September; I have not verified the counts). That points to reach, not format, as the constraint, and reach is what creators supply. Hackathon sign-ups are not exam registrations, so this supports the hypothesis without proving the lift.

Same reach, roughly 2.5× more certified, because register and attempt collapse into one moment. The Cup's $50K includes the event itself. Its creators are assumed to be separate from 1a's, so nobody is counted twice.

Dependency, before any creator is contracted: confirm with the certification team that the exam can be taken live on camera without exposing its questions, and that the platform can handle ~3,400 attempts in one session. Review and decision rule

One after-action report per event: registrations, live attempts, certified. Repeat quarterly if it beats a standard sponsored video from the same creators; otherwise stop.

Hypotheses to validate

  • Gamifying the exam engages a technical audience instead of feeling gimmicky
  • Live, same-session format lifts attempts to ~85%
  • The creator round table produces at least one lasting collaboration

Long-term
Approach 3 · Ambassador seed

Approach 3 is not scored against this year's goal, so I build no 2026 funnel for it and run it as a no-cost pilot. The certified base to build it from already exists; what it needs is time this year doesn't have. Seeding it now means 2027 doesn't start cold.

Founding cohort
The Cup's round-table creators, its top live certifiers, and a small group of the most engaged people from the existing certified base
Why they'd bother
No payment. They get public recognition as a founding Ambassador, first access to new courses and credits, and a seat on a quarterly call about upcoming course and exam changes.
How it runs
A private Discord with one informal monthly call with the Academy team; the rest is peer chat. Each member has a referral code and sees a cohort-only leaderboard, the same mechanic as the Cup. Run part-time by whoever manages the Cup creators, with no new hire.
Stop rule
If fewer than half the founding members are active in the Discord by the end of month two, stop and drop it from the 2027 plan.
2027 handoff
If the lean version shows signal, the proposal asks for a funded community manager and structured programming.

Reviewed monthly, not weekly. Hypothesis: recognition and access alone, with no paid incentive, generate organic referrals.

Tracking
How I'd track this without fooling myself
  • The one KPI every funnel is judged on: cost per certified, never a raw volume or engagement number, and never calculated until there are enough attempts to trust the ratio.
  • One code and one landing page per creator or partner. Registrations attribute to a single source instead of a creator's own estimate.
  • First-touch attribution, with a dedup rule for anyone exposed to more than one paid source. No one counts toward two funnels.
